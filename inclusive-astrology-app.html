A kundli app that never asks your gender

Published 10 August 2026 by Pacefyx, Inc. We make Muhura, so this is a page about our own app.

Muhura never asks your gender. It has no field for it. A janam kundli is cast from a date, a time and a place. Those are the three things Muhura asks for, and then it gets on with the chart.

This was not a setting somebody added later. There was never a box, so there was nothing to go back and take out.

What Muhura asks you for

Your birth date, your birth time and your birth place. That is the whole form. There is no account, no email, no phone number, and no second screen where the real questions turn up.

Nothing you type into Muhura is a demographic record. A date, a time and a place are the coordinates of a sky. They go into the kundli, the kundli goes into the reading, and none of it goes anywhere else.

Guna milan without a bride and a groom

Muhura's guna milan asks for two charts and the kind of relationship it is. It never asks which of you is which. The eight kootas are weighed the way they have always been weighed, and the arithmetic does not need to know who wore what to the wedding.

It also does not hand down a verdict. Guna milan in Muhura shows you the kootas and what each one counts for, and leaves the conclusion with you. Manglik is shown as a placement to understand rather than a judgement on a marriage, which is the same principle: describe the chart, refuse the ruling on the person.

Jyotisha is old, and a great deal of what was written around it assumed which of two people was being assessed and what she was being assessed for. Muhura keeps the technique and leaves that part behind. The grahas, the rashis, the nakshatras, the kootas and the dashas are all exactly themselves. The advice that came wrapped around them in another century is not in the app.

Relationships are named by role

You say who someone is to you, and the choices are partner, parent, child, friend, colleague and frenemy. None of those roles has a gender attached to it, and Muhura never asks who plays which part.

Two charts are two charts. Muhura reads any pair the same way, because a kundli carries no gender for it to read. What changes the reading is the kind of relationship you said it was, which is the thing that actually changes what is worth saying.

You are never asked to explain yourself to Muhura, because Muhura never asks.

There is nobody on the other end

Muhura has no astrologers to book, no consultations, no per-minute billing and no wallet to recharge. That is a pricing decision and it is also a privacy one, because a consultation is a conversation in which you explain your life to a stranger before you find out anything.

Some people want that conversation, and there are good places to have it. Muhura is for the times you would rather just read your chart.

Who finds out who you are reading about

Nobody, because Muhura never receives it. Your birth details, the people you add and every reading are worked out on your phone and stay there. There is no account to make and the App Store privacy label for Muhura reads Data Not Collected.

Matching is the sharpest version of this problem, and it is worth being plain about why. To read two charts together, an app has to know that you are reading about that person. Held on a server, that is a record of who you are seeing, sitting in a database for as long as the company lasts. Worked out on your phone, it is not a record of anything.

A field that does not exist cannot leak, cannot be subpoenaed, and cannot be sold with the company. Companies get bought. Nobody can buy what we never had, and that is a stronger protection than any promise we could make about how carefully we would have looked after it.

If you want to check this rather than take it from us, there is a separate guide to reading any astrology app's privacy label yourself, including the one thing the label does not cover.

Is Muhura in your language?

Muhura is written in six languages: English, Bangla, Gujarati, Hindi, Tamil and Telugu. Each of the five Indian languages is in its own script, not transliterated into Latin letters and not left in English with the buttons swapped.

Your kundli is read in your language, in your own script.

Being shipped in a language is not the same as being usable in it, so here is what that took. Every language was written by someone working in that language, against a glossary of the tradition's own terms, and read back by a second independent reviewer. The Sanskrit terms stay as they are, because softening a real term into something more comfortable is how you end up with an app that says nothing.

The half that usually breaks is the half you type into. A decimal comma, a year in the wrong era, digits in your own numerals: get those wrong and the app hands you a chart where every visible word is correct and the birth time is not. Muhura localises the input as carefully as the output.

Are more languages coming?

Yes. We are still adding languages, and one gets added when it can be done properly, by someone working in it. That is the only gate, and it is the reason the list grows slowly. India alone can absorb a great many more, and we know it.

Why Muhura was built this way

Muhura was thought up in the Bay Area, where you assume as a matter of course that the person next to you might be anyone, and that a form which demands they pick a side is a badly designed form. So the questions never got asked in the first place. There was no review at the end of the project to take them out, because there was nothing left to take out.

That is also just better engineering. A field you do not collect is a field you cannot get wrong, cannot lose, and never have to defend. The design that respects people the most turned out to be the design with the fewest moving parts, which is a nice thing to be able to report.

If you have ever opened a kundli app and been asked, before anything else, to say whether you were the boy or the girl: this one does not ask.
